\begin{frame}{Backup: tie-strength tradeoff}
\begin{softbox}[title={Reviewer concern}]
\footnotesize If complex knowledge transfer requires \emph{strong} ties, why do structural holes --- which supply \emph{weak/distant} ties --- predict atypical combinations?
\end{softbox}
\vspace{0.3cm}
\begin{accentbox}[title={Response}]
\footnotesize Atypical CPC \textbf{pairing} is an \HL{access} problem, not a transfer problem. The binding constraint is \emph{seeing} the distant combination, not executing the knowledge exchange once seen.\\
\vspace{2pt}
\color{muted}\textit{Tension acknowledged:} diverse ties $\to$ novel info; strong ties $\to$ bandwidth for complex transfer (Aral \& Van Alstyne 2011; Levin \& Cross 2004). Knowledge transfer intensity remains an open moderator for future work.
\end{accentbox}
\end{frame}
