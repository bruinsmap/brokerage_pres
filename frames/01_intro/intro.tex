\begin{frame}{Brokerage and Innovation}
\footnotesize
\begin{ghostquote}
``Holes in social structure create a competitive advantage for people whose relationships span the holes.''\ \normalfont\color{muted}---~Burt (2004)
\end{ghostquote}
\vspace{-0.25cm}
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\column{0.54\textwidth}
\begin{softbox}[title=The research puzzle]
Most patents recombine technologies conventionally. We focus on the smaller set that combines knowledge in \textbf{statistically unusual} ways and ask \textbf{who is structurally positioned} to make those combinations happen.
\end{softbox}
\vspace{0.01cm}
\begin{softbox}[title=Why brokerage should matter]
Inventors bridging disconnected neighborhoods see more non-redundant knowledge and more opportunities to connect ideas usually kept apart.
\end{softbox}

\column{0.43\textwidth}
\begin{statbox}[title=What is at stake?]
\textbf{Theory:} micro-foundations of novelty\\[0.03cm]
\textbf{Firms:} do cross-boundary brokers help?\\[0.03cm]
\textbf{Regions:} does local variety amplify brokerage?
\end{statbox}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The conference takeaway in one view}
\small
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\column{0.31\textwidth}
\begin{softbox}[title={\stepchip{1}\;Question}]
Do brokers generate more \textbf{atypical patents} than inventors in denser collaboration neighborhoods?
\end{softbox}

\column{0.31\textwidth}
\begin{softbox}[title={\stepchip{2}\;Setting}]
EU-28 patenting, 1984--2023, inventor-level panel, time-varying networks, CPC-pair rarity.
\end{softbox}

\column{0.34\textwidth}
\begin{softbox}[title={\stepchip{3}\;Contribution}]
We bring regional brokerage down to the inventor level, showing how organizational boundaries and historical periods reshape the effect.
\end{softbox}
\end{columns}

\vspace{0.15cm}
\takeaway{More brokerage should raise the probability of atypical recombination because bridging positions expose inventors to combinations others never encounter.}
\end{frame}
